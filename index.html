Journal Article • The Journal of Supercomputing (2025)

SimpDepth: a simple CNN-transformer architecture for self-supervised monocular depth estimation in autonomous vehicles

Arman Forouzesh, Amin Nikoofard

Accurate depth perception is essential for the safe navigation of autonomous vehicles. While modern deep neural networks have demonstrated impressive depth estimation performance, real-world onboard deployment is often hindered by high computational overhead. In this work, we present SimpDepth, an efficient hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture designed for self-supervised monocular depth estimation. SimpDepth delivers strong feature extraction fidelity while remaining lightweight and viable for real-time edge execution in robotic and autonomous vehicular platforms.

Computer Vision & Medical Imaging

Deep Learning based MS Lesion Segmentation in Brain MRI

Multiple Sclerosis (MS) is an autoimmune inflammatory disorder of the central nervous system characterized by demyelinating white matter (WM) lesions in the brain and spinal cord. FLAIR (Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery) MRI sequences selectively suppress the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) hyperintensity, allowing high sensitivity observation of plaques.

Manual or semi-automated segmentation is clinically laborious, error-prone, and subject to considerable inter-observer variance. To address this, deep convolutional architectures were deployed to segment lesions across the four recognized McDonald Criteria anatomical distributions:

  • Periventricular lesions: Abutting the lateral ventricles.
  • Juxtacortical lesions: Interfacing the cerebral cortex surface.
  • Infratentorial lesions: In the brainstem and cerebellum, influencing balance and motor control.
  • Deep white matter lesions: Subcortical regions associated with cognitive impairment.

Explainable AI (XAI) & Medical CT

Deep Learning based CT Segmentation for Bone Erosion Detection in Sinusitis

Bone erosion in sinusitis patients indicates aggressive pathologies such as invasive fungal infections. Detecting erosion early is vital for preoperative planning to prevent catastrophic complications, including intra-orbital penetration and injury to the internal carotid artery or optic nerve.

By combining high-resolution Computed Tomography (CT) segmentation backbones with Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) via Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM), the network produces visual heatmaps verifying that its predictive attention localizes strictly to anatomically critical sinus walls and opacified cavities rather than spurious artifacts, securing clinical interpretability and trust.

Robotics & Non-Linear Control

Design & Evaluation of PID and Fuzzy Logic Controllers for a 3R Non-Planar Robot

This investigation models and controls a 3-degree-of-freedom (3-DOF) non-planar robotic manipulator governed by non-linear ordinary differential equations (ODEs), accounting for link masses, lengths, and variable payload forces. The mission objective was to synthesize joint control torques to execute accurate Cartesian trajectory tracking from initial coordinates (1.5, 0, 1.5) to target (0, 0, 3) within 10 seconds.

Figure 3: Kinematic and dynamic schematic model of the 3R non-planar robot.

Control Methodologies:

  • PID Controller: Tuned with parameters \(K_p = 50\), \(K_i = 50\), and \(K_d = 5\) with torque saturation limiters.
  • Fuzzy Logic Controller (FLC): Designed with dual inputs (angular error \(e_\theta\) and angular speed error \(e_\omega\)), 5 input membership functions, 7 output membership functions, and 25 rule bases requiring optimization of 42 parameters.

Multivariable Control Systems

Hankel-Norm Interaction Index for Multivariable Control Configuration

In multivariable decentralized control, the classical Relative Gain Array (RGA) relies solely on steady-state gain matrices, often recommending input-output pairings that destabilize the closed-loop system under dynamic conditions. The Hankel-norm interaction index incorporates controllability and observability Gramians (\(\Gamma_c, \Gamma_o\)) to provide a dynamic measure of subsystem interactions.

Figure 6: Step response comparison showing Hankel-norm pairing (Blue/Solid) delivering superior settling time and stability over classical static RGA (Red/Dashed).
