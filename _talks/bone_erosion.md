### Bone Erosion and CT 

Bone erosion is often associated with aggressive or chronic forms of sinusitis, especially allergic fungal rhinosinusitis and invasive fungal infections. Detecting erosion enables clinicians to better evaluate disease severity and predict complications, such as orbital or intracranial extension. Effective segmentation in CT images provides reliable information for preoperative evaluation, guides surgical planning, and helps avoid damage to critical adjacent structures like the optic nerve or carotid artery.
Computed Tomography (CT) is the preferred modality for diagnosing sinus diseases because of its high sensitivity and spatial resolution and its ability to detect bone tissues. However, manual diagnosis and analysis of complex pathological features by skilled radiologists is a time-consuming, laborious, and expertise-intensive process. Deep learning approaches address these limitations by providing automation and efficiency, demonstrating good performance in learning meaningful information and radiomic features, which traditional machine learning models often struggle to extract from raw imaging data. 
A key benefit of using advanced DL frameworks for segmentation is the ability to incorporate Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques, specifically Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM), which is vital for clinical adoption. XAI enhances model transparency and interpretability by providing visual explanations that highlight the specific areas in the image influencing the model's prediction. Grad-CAM visualizations confirm that the model’s attention is appropriately localized to anatomically meaningful regions, such as sinus walls and opacified cavities, validating the model’s reliability. This capability allows medical practitioners to assess the clinical relevance of the automated segmentation results, thereby fostering the trust required to integrate these powerful tools into routine medical practice for precise and timely diagnosis.
